|---|---|---|
| **Organic** (video on your channel) | YouTube's ranking + suggestion engine | **Everything.** This is where SEO lives |
| **Paid** (Google Ads / Meta) | Your targeting, bid and creative | **Almost nothing.** The ad is served to an audience you buy |

So do both, and do them separately. Sections 1–3 make the video findable forever, for free.

## 4. Google Ads — and the thing to fix first

### ⚠️ You cannot currently optimise for anything except installs

`MobileApp/package.json` has **no analytics or attribution SDK** — no Firebase, no GA4, no MMP.
`services/analytics.ts` posts events to your own server, which is excellent for your dashboards and
completely invisible to Google and Meta.

That matters because of what your own database says:

> **200 users. 51 have a parsed résumé.** A 25.5% activation rate — three out of four people who
> sign up never get far enough to see what the app does.

An install-optimised campaign buys you *more of exactly that*, because "installed" is the only
signal it can see, and it will happily find the cheapest people who install and stop. You would be
paying to grow the 75%.

**The fix is small and it is the highest-leverage thing on this page.** The Firebase project already
exists — `GoogleService-Info.plist` and `google-services.json` are both sitting in `MobileApp/`. What
is missing is the SDK and the event:

1. Add Firebase Analytics to the app (config plugin + `googleServicesFile` for **both** platforms —
   right now only Android is wired in `app.json`).
2. Log one event where value is first proven: `resume_uploaded`. Add `first_letter_generated` and
   `application_submitted` when you can.
3. Link Firebase → Google Ads, and import those as conversions.
4. Switch the campaign from *Install volume* to *In-app actions*.

Until that ships, keep spend deliberately small. You are buying installs you cannot yet grade.

### Campaign structure

App campaigns don't take placements or keywords — you feed them assets and a target, and Google
distributes across YouTube, Search, Play and Display. So the levers you actually have are
**geography, budget, and creative.**

| Setting | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Campaign type | **App → App installs** now; **App engagement/In-app actions** once events land |
| Split | **One campaign per platform.** iOS is SKAdNetwork-limited and reports differently; mixing them makes both unreadable |
| Bidding | Target CPI. Set the daily budget to **at least 50× your target CPI** or the campaign never leaves learning |
| Geo | **Not worldwide.** See below |
| Language | English (add others only when you localise the store listing) |

### Geography — split it, don't blend it

Your country data is too sparse to be conclusive (only 9 of 200 users have a country recorded), so
treat this as a starting hypothesis and let activation rate settle it:

- **Group A — high-intent English:** US, UK, Canada, Australia, Ireland. Higher CPI, and the
  country-format feature (US résumé vs UK CV) is a real differentiator here.
- **Group B — volume:** India, Pakistan, Egypt, and similar. Much cheaper installs; this is where
  your known users actually are.

Run them as **separate campaigns with separate budgets**, or Group B's cheap installs will eat the
whole budget and Group A will never get data. **Judge each on activation rate, not CPI** — a ₹20
install that never uploads a résumé costs more than a $3 one that does.

Add UTM tags to the links so your own analytics can separate YouTube traffic from everything else:


Connect gives you campaign links under App Analytics — worth setting up before the first upload
